How a customer gets scored

Every prospect starts at zero and earns or loses points across five buckets. The score is capped 0–100. Everything that moved the number shows up as a chip on the customer's card, so an agent always knows why the system put someone in front of them.

1. Timing — are they due? (up to +40)

  • Recurring service past due +32 — they are on the books for a visit we have not delivered.
  • Recurring service due inside 30 days +28.
  • 10–16 months since last tune-up +30 — the annual window.
  • 16+ months +24 · 26+ months +16 · never had a tune-up +18.
  • Last job 6–18 months ago +8 (still warm). Older than 4 years −6 (stale number, stale relationship).

2. Value — are they worth the dial? (up to +25)

  • Close rate 60%+ on 2+ opportunities +10 · 40–60% +6.
  • Average ticket $2,000+ +8 · $800+ +5.
  • Top 10% lifetime revenue +7 · 3+ completed jobs +4.
  • Has financed with us before +5 · pre-approved credit on file +6.

3. Fit & cross-sell (up to +15)

  • Failed hardness test +8 · failed chlorine test +6 — water treatment leads straight from ServiceTitan tags.
  • HVAC-only customer +5 — we have never done their plumbing.
  • System 15+ years +10 · 10–14 years +6 · VIP tag +5 · SmartAC +3.

4. Risk — reasons to think twice (down to −45)

  • 1★ review −35 · 2★ −22 · 3★ −8. Softened by 55% if they kept buying after the review, and by 40% if it is over three years old.
  • Complaint tasks — Fraud/Claims/Damaged Property −20, Management Follow-Up −12, Driving Complaint & Sales Promises −10, Refund −8, Financing Concern −6. Multiplied by how long it sat open: closed in 2 days ×0.5, a week ×0.8, a month ×1.0, over a month or still open ×1.5–1.6. Each extra complaint −5.
  • Chronic canceller — 40%+ cancellation rate on 2+ jobs −18 · 20–40% −10 · 3+ cancellations −8.
  • Credit declined previously −10 · recall rate over 30% −6 · warranty rate over 30% −4.

5. Recovery — the sleeper bucket (+12)

If a customer had a complaint, we closed it inside a week, and they came back and bought afterward, they earn +12. These are the most under-called people in the database: they had a problem, we made it right, and they proved their loyalty with their wallet. They belong on the phone, not in the penalty box.

Who never appears

Anyone tagged Do Not Call, Do Not Service, Not In Service, opt-out or flagged Do Not Service in ServiceTitan is removed from the file entirely — they are not filtered out in the browser, they are never loaded. Customers with no phone number, no completed job, or outside the Las Vegas metro are also excluded. Customers tagged Do NOT Sell Monthly Club stay callable but carry a warning chip so nobody pitches them a membership.

How the floor gets its calls

Stuart sets the list each morning. He picks a starting list, tightens it however he wants — score, membership, how long since a tune-up, system age, zip codes, whether they have ever complained — and the panel tells him how many match, how many that is per agent, and how many rounds the list will hold. When he hits Distribute he gets a short code and a link to drop in Teams.

Agents paste the code in the bar at the top, or just click Stuart's link. Your calls appear under My Calls. Work them; when you run out, ask Stuart for the next round — he bumps the round number, resends, and you get the next block with nothing repeated.

Why two agents never get the same customer

The code carries the criteria, the block size and the round — and everyone's copy of the file is identical, so every browser deals exactly the same hand. The list is sorted by score and then dealt like cards: agent 1 takes the 1st, agent 2 the 2nd, on down the room and around again. Same code in, same disjoint split out, on all thirteen machines. Nobody needs to be online at the same time and nothing has to sync.

It is also fair by construction. Cutting the deck would hand the first agent the best hundred and the last agent the leftovers; dealing it gives every agent the same average score — measured at 61 across all thirteen on a typical list.

What happens to your work

Bookings are kept under your name and survive closing the browser. Anyone you dial but do not book is marked called and drops out of your lists for seven days. "End shift" hands back anything you never got to, so it is first up in your next block. Bookings become real jobs in ServiceTitan, so tomorrow's rebuild drops them from the list on its own — the nightly rebuild is the sync.

Stuart / supervisors: the Distribute tab shows a per-agent breakdown before you commit — how many calls each person gets, their average score, best and lowest. Every agent's block can be exported to CSV from there if anyone wants theirs on paper. Choosing Supervisor — view all gives the same tools without being dealt a block.
